## Elephants in Angul

Angul district is part of the range of the Asian elephant in Odisha, and the Satkosia area falls in the Mahanadi Elephant Reserve. Elephants live in herds led by the oldest female, called the matriarch. A herd is usually made of mothers, daughters, aunts and young calves. Adult males often move alone or in small groups.

An adult elephant can eat more than 150 kilograms of grass, leaves, bark, bamboo and fruit in a single day, and needs to drink a lot of water. To find enough food, elephants walk long distances along old routes called elephant corridors. When forests are cut or broken up by roads, mines, canals and settlements, elephants sometimes enter villages and crop fields, and conflict with people increases.

Elephants are very important for the forest. They spread seeds of many trees through their dung, open up paths in thick forest and dig for water that other animals also use. That is why the elephant is sometimes called a gardener of the forest.

## How to stay safe near elephants

- Never go close to an elephant or a herd, and never try to take a selfie with a wild elephant.
- Do not throw stones, burst crackers, shout or chase elephants. This makes them angry and dangerous.
- Keep a safe distance, at least a few hundred metres, and leave the elephants a clear path to move away.
- Avoid walking in the forest or on forest roads in the early morning, late evening and at night, when elephants are most active.
- If elephants are seen near a village, inform the local forest office or forest staff immediately, and keep away until they arrive.
- Do not keep country liquor, mahua or ripe fruits stored inside houses in elephant areas, because the smell attracts elephants.
- Never put up illegal electric fences or live wires. They kill elephants and are a punishable crime.
- If an elephant charges, do not run in a straight line on open ground; move away quickly, behind large trees or uphill if possible, and drop any bag or cloth that may distract it.

## Compensation

When people suffer loss of life, injury, or damage to crops or houses because of wild animals, the Government of Odisha provides compensation through the Forest Department. People should report such incidents to the nearest forest office as soon as possible.
