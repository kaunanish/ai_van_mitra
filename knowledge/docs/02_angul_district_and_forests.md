## About Angul

Angul is a district in central Odisha. It became a separate district on 1 April 1993; before that its areas were part of Dhenkanal district. The district covers about 6,232 square kilometres and its headquarters is Angul town, about 150 kilometres from Bhubaneswar. The district is also written as Anugul.

The name Angul is linked by local tradition to the word "Anugol". According to legend, the last Khond chief of the area was named Anu, and he was defeated in a struggle called "gol". The Khond, Savara and Gond communities have lived in this region for a very long time.

Angul is also an important industrial district of Odisha, known for coal mining, aluminium and steel industries. Because of this, protecting the remaining forests, rivers and wildlife of Angul is especially important.

## Forest administration

The forests of Angul district are looked after by three forest divisions: Angul Forest Division, Satkosia Wildlife Division and Athamallik Forest Division. Each division is headed by a Divisional Forest Officer, also called the DFO. The forest divisions protect forests and wildlife, raise new plantations, prevent forest fires, reduce conflict between people and elephants, and work with village communities through forest protection committees. The Divisional Forest Officer (DFO) of the Angul Forest Division is Nitish Kumar.

## Types of forests

Most of the forests in this region are tropical deciduous forests. Deciduous means that the trees shed their leaves in the dry season, mostly between February and April, to save water, and grow fresh leaves before the monsoon. Sal is the most important tree of these forests. Other common trees include Asan, Mahua, Kendu, Bija (Indian kino), Harida, Bahada, Amla, Kusum, Arjun and bamboo.

## Forests and people

Forests give people many things besides timber. These are called non-timber forest produce, and they include Kendu leaves, Mahua flowers and seeds, Sal leaves and seeds, honey, tamarind, bamboo, broom grass and medicinal plants. Odisha is one of India's largest producers of Kendu leaves, which are used to roll bidis, and leaf collection gives seasonal work to many families living near forests.

Forests also protect the soil from erosion, keep streams and rivers flowing, give shelter to wildlife, keep the air clean and store carbon, which helps fight climate change.
## Animals found in the forests

The forests of Angul district, especially the Satkosia Tiger Reserve, are home to many kinds of animals. These are some of the animals found here.

Mammals:
- Asian elephant
- Tiger (now very rare here)
- Leopard
- Sloth bear
- Indian wild dog, also called dhole
- Striped hyena
- Jungle cat and leopard cat
- Gaur, also called Indian bison
- Sambar deer
- Chital, also called spotted deer
- Barking deer
- Four-horned antelope, called chousingha
- Indian mouse deer
- Wild boar
- Hanuman langur
- Indian giant squirrel
- Indian porcupine
- Indian pangolin

Reptiles:
- Gharial
- Mugger crocodile
- Indian python
- Freshwater turtles of the Mahanadi, such as the Indian narrow-headed softshell turtle

Birds:
- Indian peafowl
- Red junglefowl
- Malabar pied hornbill and Indian grey hornbill
- Indian roller
- Crested serpent eagle
- Stork-billed kingfisher
