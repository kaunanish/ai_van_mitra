# Common Trees and Birds of the Angul Forests

## Sal

Sal (Shorea robusta) is the most important tree of the forests of Odisha. It is a tall tree with hard, strong wood. Sal leaves are stitched together to make leaf plates and cups, which are a natural replacement for plastic plates. Sal seeds are collected by forest communities and the seed oil has many uses. Sal trees drop many of their leaves in the dry season and grow new leaves quickly, so the forest stays green for most of the year.

## Mahua

Mahua (Madhuca longifolia) is a large tree loved by forest communities. Its sweet, juicy flowers fall in the early summer and are collected in the morning to be eaten, dried and sold. Its seeds give an oil used for cooking and lighting. Many animals, including bears, deer and birds, also eat mahua flowers.

## Kendu

Kendu (Diospyros melanoxylon) is a small tree whose leaves are used to roll bidis. It gives seasonal income to many families. Its fruit is eaten by people, birds and animals.

## Other useful trees

Harida, Bahada and Amla are three trees whose fruits are used together in the traditional medicine called Triphala. Arjun trees grow along rivers and streams and their bark is used in traditional medicine. Bija, also called Indian kino, gives a red gum. Asan trees are used to rear tasar silkworms. Bamboo grows fast and is used for building, baskets and many crafts.

## Odisha state symbols

The state animal of Odisha is the Sambar deer. The state bird of Odisha is the Indian roller, also called the blue jay, known for its brilliant blue wings in flight. The state tree is the Ashwatha, or peepal, and the state flower is the Ashoka.

## Birds of the forest

More than 200 species of birds have been recorded in the Satkosia area. Birds commonly seen in these forests include the Indian peafowl, red junglefowl, Indian grey hornbill, Indian roller, various owls, kingfishers along rivers, and birds of prey such as the crested serpent eagle. River birds like cormorants, egrets and herons are seen along the Mahanadi. Many migratory birds visit the rivers and wetlands in winter.

## Why birds matter

Birds spread seeds, pollinate flowers and eat insects that can harm crops. Owls and birds of prey control rats in fields. A forest with many kinds of birds is usually a healthy forest.
