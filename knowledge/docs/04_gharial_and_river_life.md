# The Gharial and River Life of the Mahanadi

## What is a gharial?

The gharial is a crocodile with a very long, thin snout. It is found only in the rivers of the Indian subcontinent. The thin snout is perfect for catching fish, which is almost the only food of an adult gharial, and gharials are generally shy and not a danger to people. Adult males grow a round lump at the tip of the snout called a "ghara", named after an Indian clay pot. That is how the gharial got its name.

The gharial is listed as Critically Endangered, which means it faces a very high risk of disappearing from the wild. Its numbers fell because of sand mining, dams, fishing nets in which it gets trapped, and the loss of the sandbanks it needs for nesting and basking.

## Gharials in Satkosia

The Mahanadi River in the Satkosia Gorge is the natural home of gharials in Odisha. A gharial research and conservation unit works at Tikarpada. Gharial eggs are collected and hatched safely, and the young are reared until they are big enough, and then released back into the Mahanadi. This method is called "rear and release".

## The mugger crocodile

The mugger, or marsh crocodile, also lives in the Mahanadi. It has a broad, strong snout and eats fish, birds and mammals. Unlike the gharial, a mugger can be dangerous to people and cattle, so visitors must never go into the water or close to the river bank where muggers are seen.

## Freshwater turtles and fish

Rare freshwater turtles, including softshell turtles, live in the Mahanadi in Satkosia. Turtles help keep rivers clean by eating dead fish and plants. About 183 species of fish have been recorded in the reserve.

## How you can help river life

Do not throw plastic or waste into rivers. Do not buy turtle meat or turtle shells; hunting and trading turtles is a crime. Report illegal fishing with poison or explosives, and illegal sand mining, to the forest department.
