# Forest Fire Prevention and Rules for Visitors

## Forest fires

Most forest fires in Odisha happen in the dry season, from about February to June, when fallen leaves on the forest floor become dry. Almost all forest fires are caused by people, for example by burning leaves to collect mahua flowers easily, throwing away burning bidis or matchsticks, or fires spreading from fields. Forest fires kill small animals, birds' eggs and young plants, damage the soil and fill the air with smoke.

## How to prevent forest fires

- Never throw a lit bidi, cigarette or matchstick in or near a forest.
- Do not light fires inside the forest. Use only the fire places allowed by forest staff at camps.
- When collecting mahua flowers, sweep the ground clean instead of burning the leaves.
- If you see a forest fire, inform the nearest forest office, the forest protection committee of your village, or forest staff immediately.

## Rules for visitors in forests and protected areas

- Enter protected areas only through the official gates and with the required permits, and only during the permitted hours.
- Stay on the marked roads and trails, and always follow the instructions of forest staff and guides.
- Do not feed, tease or disturb wild animals, and keep a safe distance from them.
- Keep silence in the forest. Loud music, horns and shouting disturb animals.
- Do not carry plastic, and carry all your waste back with you.
- Do not pluck plants or flowers, and do not collect anything from the forest.
- Hunting, carrying weapons, and harming any wild animal are serious crimes under the Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972.
- Do not drink alcohol in protected areas.

## Be a friend of the forest

Plant trees and look after them. Save paper and water. Say no to plastic. Never buy products made from wild animals, such as skins, ivory, horns or turtle shells. Tell others what you learnt about forests and wildlife today.
