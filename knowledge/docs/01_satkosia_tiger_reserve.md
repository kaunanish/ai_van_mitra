# Satkosia Tiger Reserve

## Overview

Satkosia Tiger Reserve lies in central Odisha. It was declared a tiger reserve in 2007 by joining two older wildlife sanctuaries: the Satkosia Gorge Wildlife Sanctuary, created in 1976, and the Baisipalli Wildlife Sanctuary, notified in 1981. The reserve covers about 964 square kilometres, of which about 524 square kilometres is the core area.

The reserve spreads across four districts of Odisha: Angul, Cuttack, Nayagarh and Boudh. Angul town is about 60 kilometres away from the reserve. The reserve is also part of the Mahanadi Elephant Reserve.

## The Satkosia Gorge and its name

The heart of the reserve is the Satkosia Gorge, where the Mahanadi River cuts through the hills of the Eastern Ghats. The gorge is about 22 kilometres long. The name Satkosia comes from "sat", meaning seven, and "kosh", an old unit of distance equal to about two miles. So Satkosia means a stretch of about seven kosh, or fourteen miles, which is the length of the gorge.

Satkosia is special because two great natural regions meet here: the Deccan Peninsula and the Eastern Ghats. Because of this meeting, plants and animals of both regions are found together, which makes the area very rich in biodiversity. The Satkosia Gorge has also been recognised as a Ramsar site, which means it is a wetland of international importance.

## Forests and plants

The forests of Satkosia are mainly mixed deciduous forests, with Sal trees as one of the most important species, along with riverine forests along the Mahanadi. More than 400 species of plants have been recorded in the reserve.

## Animals of Satkosia

Satkosia is home to many mammals, including the Asian elephant, leopard, sloth bear, gaur (Indian bison), sambar, chital (spotted deer), barking deer, nilgai, the four-horned antelope called chousingha, and the tiny Indian mouse deer. More than 200 species of birds and about 183 species of fish have been recorded here.

The river in the gorge is home to the gharial and the mugger crocodile. Rare freshwater turtles also live in the Mahanadi here, and turtles and crocodiles can often be seen basking on sandbanks in the winter sun.

## Tigers in Satkosia

The tiger population in Satkosia has fallen sharply over the years. In 2018, Satkosia was chosen for India's first inter-state tiger relocation project. A male tiger was brought from Kanha Tiger Reserve and a tigress from Bandhavgarh Tiger Reserve, both in Madhya Pradesh. The relocation did not succeed, and protecting the habitat and the prey animals remains the most important task for bringing tigers back.

## Visiting Satkosia

Tikarpada, on the banks of the Mahanadi, is the main eco-tourism centre of Satkosia. It has an interpretation centre, a gharial research and conservation unit, nature trails and camping facilities. Other places known to visitors include Chhotkei, which has a community-run nature camp, and Purunakote, where chital and other animals are often seen.

Winter, from November to mid-February, is the most pleasant time to visit. Animals are easier to see in the hot summer months, from mid-February to mid-June, when they gather near water. During the monsoon the Mahanadi can flood, so visitors must follow the advice of forest staff.
